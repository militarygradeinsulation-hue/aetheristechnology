## The One Thing That Drives Everything

AI engines don't rank websites. They surface **entities they trust as authoritative answers** to specific questions.

To become an authority in an AI engine you must:
1. Be a **clearly defined entity** (the AI knows exactly what Aetheris is)
2. Produce **answer-shaped content** (written to answer the exact questions buyers ask)
3. Build a **citation network** (other credible sources mention and link to you)
4. Demonstrate **original authority** (data, research, and expertise nobody else has)
5. Maintain **cross-platform consistency** (same entity, same description, everywhere)

When all five are present, AI engines begin surfacing Aetheris not because you gamed an algorithm but because you are genuinely the most credible, consistent, answer-ready source on the topic. That is the only version of this that compounds and lasts.

## Part 2 — Engine-Specific Rules

Each AI engine has a different retrieval backend and different weighting signals. Here is what matters per engine and the specific rules for each.

---

### ChatGPT (OpenAI)
**How it retrieves:** Bing-powered web search for real-time answers. Also trained on web data through its knowledge cutoff. GPT-4o and GPT-5 use real-time Bing retrieval for most business queries.

**What it weights:**
- Named sources with domain authority
- Answer-first content structure (answer in the first 150 words)
- Specific, verifiable data points (numbers, named findings, dated stats)
- Original research and unique data
- Consistency of entity across indexed sources

**Rules for ChatGPT:**
1. Every page must answer a specific question in the first 150 words — not a preamble, the actual answer.
2. Include specific numbers everywhere. "Businesses lose an average of X leads per month to broken follow-up" beats "businesses lose many leads."
3. Structure content with question-format H2 headings that mirror exactly how a buyer types a query.
4. Build Bing authority — submit to Bing Webmaster Tools, enable IndexNow, claim Bing Places.
5. Every data point you publish should be something nobody else has published — original scan data, original findings, original benchmarks.

---

### Claude (Anthropic)
**How it retrieves:** Trained on web data through knowledge cutoff; uses web search for current queries. Values: accuracy, nuanced explanation, credentialed sourcing, original thought.

**What it weights:**
- Credentialed, named authorship (Experience + Expertise signals)
- Thorough, accurate explanations over keyword-stuffed content
- Original frameworks, named methodologies, and defined categories
- Long-form, well-structured content that explains concepts clearly
- Sources that are cited by other credible sources

**Rules for Claude:**
1. Every pillar piece must be comprehensive — not 500 words but 2,000+ words covering the topic from every angle.
2. Name your methodology. "The Revenue Forensics Framework" is a named, citable thing. "Business diagnostic process" is not.
3. Define the category explicitly on a dedicated page — "What is Revenue Forensics?" This is the content Claude will pull from when someone asks about the topic.
4. Write in first-person expertise where it matters: "In my experience running forensic scans across [X type of business]..." Named experience beats anonymous advice.
5. Create a glossary of Revenue Forensics terms — Leak Register, Revenue Score, Case File, Active Case. When you define the vocabulary of a category, the AI learns your definitions.

---

### Gemini (Google)
**How it retrieves:** Google's full index. E-E-A-T signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness). Schema markup. Google Business Profile. YouTube content.

**What it weights:**
- E-E-A-T — who wrote this, what are their credentials, are they cited elsewhere
- Structured data / schema markup (the richest signal in Google's ecosystem)
- Google Business Profile completeness and review volume
- Content freshness — regularly updated content outperforms stale
- Backlinks from authoritative domains
- YouTube videos (Google owns YouTube; video content surfaces in AI summaries)

**Rules for Gemini:**
1. Every page needs full JSON-LD structured data — Organization, Product, Person, FAQPage, HowTo where applicable. This is the single highest-leverage technical action for Gemini.
2. Publish a YouTube video for every major content piece — even a 3-minute "here is what we found" scan walkthrough. Google surfaces YouTube heavily in AI answers.
3. Keep Google Business Profile 100% complete with fresh posts, photos, and responded reviews.
4. Update pillar content regularly — add a "Last verified: [date]" line at the top of every article.
5. Build legitimate backlinks from Indiana business publications, manufacturing industry blogs, and RevOps-adjacent sites. Unlinked mentions matter too — Gemini reads the whole web.

---

### Perplexity
**How it retrieves:** Real-time web search + synthesis. Perplexity is extremely citation-heavy — it shows 4–8 sources per answer and users click through. Getting cited in Perplexity drives real referral traffic.

**What it weights:**
- Recency — Perplexity heavily favors recently published or updated content
- Specificity — answers with exact numbers, exact process steps, exact tool names surface over vague content
- Direct answer quality — the page that most directly answers the question wins
- Domain clarity — pages about one specific topic beat generalist sites

**Rules for Perplexity:**
1. Publish new content on a consistent schedule — Perplexity favors fresh. Every new piece is an indexing event.
2. Every article needs a "Quick Answer" box at the very top — a 2–3 sentence direct answer before any context. Perplexity extracts these.
3. Use exact, specific language in headings: "How much does a website audit cost?" not "Website Audit Pricing."
4. Create dedicated comparison pages — "Revenue Forensics vs. Marketing Agencies," "Revenue Score vs. SEO Audit," "Aetheris vs. Hiring a Consultant." Perplexity surfaces comparison content heavily.
5. Publish data — any original research with a specific number gets cited. "73% of businesses we scanned had no tracking pixels" is a citable fact. "Many businesses lack tracking" is not.

---

### Meta AI
**How it retrieves:** Bing-powered web retrieval + owned Meta surfaces (Facebook Pages, Instagram profiles, public posts, product catalogs, reviews). Already covered in the Meta AI Optimization Plan — refer to that document for Meta-specific rules.

**Key additional rule for Meta:** Answer-shaped captions on every Instagram and Facebook post. Meta AI reads social captions as content. Write them like articles, not hashtag dumps.

### Grok (xAI / X)

**Rules for Grok:**

## Part 3 — The Universal Content Rules
### 12 Rules That Apply to Every Engine, Every Piece of Content

**Rule 1 — Answer First, Always.**
The answer to the question the content addresses must appear in the first 150 words. Not a preamble. Not "in this article we will explore." The answer. AI engines extract opening content heavily.

**Rule 2 — Question-Format Headings.**
Every H2 and H3 must be phrased as the exact question a buyer types. "How do I find where my business is losing revenue?" not "Revenue Loss Detection." The question format is how AI matches content to queries.

**Rule 3 — Named Author With Credentials, Every Time.**
Every piece of content must have Joseph Toney's name and a one-line credential — "Founder, Aetheris | Revenue Forensics | Marine Corps Veteran | MS Marketing, Liberty University." Anonymous content earns zero authority signals. AI engines are increasingly filtering for named, credentialed authorship.

**Rule 4 — Specific Numbers or Don't Bother.**
Every claim must have a number attached. Not "most businesses lose leads." "Businesses we've scanned lose an average of 47% of inbound leads to response-time gaps over 24 hours." If you don't have the data yet, say "in our experience" and give a range. Vague claims are filtered out of AI summaries.

**Rule 5 — Define Your Category Vocabulary Explicitly.**
On the site and in content, define every Revenue Forensics term plainly: "A Revenue Leak is a specific, named gap in a business's operations where money is being lost — not a general weakness, a findable, fixable, measurable hole." When you define the vocabulary, AI engines learn your definitions and use them. This is how you become the source.

**Rule 6 — Freshness Dating.**
Every page and article must show a "Last updated: [date]" timestamp. Freshness is a universal retrieval signal across all engines.

**Rule 7 — Original Data Over Generic Claims.**
One piece of content built around your own real scan data — "across 47 businesses we scanned in Q1 2026, the most common critical leak was..." — is worth more for AI authority than 50 posts of general advice. Publish real data as fast as you can accumulate it.

**Rule 8 — Cross-link Intentionally.**
Every article links to at least two other Aetheris pages using exact-match anchor text ("Revenue Score," "Leak Register," "Revenue Forensics audit"). This builds topical authority internally before external citations arrive.

**Rule 9 — One Topic Per Page.**
Never try to rank for everything on one page. "Revenue Forensics" gets one pillar page. "How to find revenue leaks" gets one article. "What is a Revenue Score" gets one page. Specificity per URL is how AI engines learn what you're the authority on.

**Rule 10 — Cite Your Sources and Be Cited.**
Every statistic you reference from external sources gets a citation. Every piece of original data you publish is designed to be cited by others. Citations are the currency of AI authority — both giving and receiving.

**Rule 11 — Schema On Every Page.**
JSON-LD structured data is machine-readable metadata that tells AI crawlers exactly what each page is. An article about revenue leaks with FAQPage schema becomes a direct answer candidate. A tool page with Product schema becomes a purchasable offering in AI product results. This is non-negotiable.

**Rule 12 — Repurpose Into Every Format.**
One pillar piece of written content becomes: a LinkedIn article, a short video walkthrough, an Instagram carousel, a podcast pitch, a quote tweet. AI engines index every format. Being present in multiple formats on multiple platforms compounds the entity signal.
